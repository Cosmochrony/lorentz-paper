  \abstract{
  This note settles one question about a posited kernel: whether the local covariant four-derivative
  transverse-traceless kernel $\OTT = \Box\,(\cEH + \beta\Box)$ deforms the dispersion of the graviton.
  It does not.
  The kernel is an assumption, not a consequence of the projective spectral action: a Lorentzian counterpart of
  that action requires an in-in construction on a closed time path, which is open. Every statement here is
  conditional on the posit.

  When $\cEH \neq 0$ the two factors are coprime, and the solution space is the \emph{direct sum}
  $\ker\Box \oplus \ker(\cEH + \beta\Box)$: every solution decomposes uniquely into a summand annihilated by
  $\Box$ and a summand annihilated by $\cEH + \beta\Box$.
  The second summand is a massive sector only when $\beta \neq 0$; for $\beta = 0$ it is trivial and the
  decomposition degenerates to $\ker\Box$.
  Whenever the second summand is nontrivial, a generic solution satisfies neither factor equation on its own.
  In units with $c = 1$ the massless summand obeys $\omega^2 = |\vec k|^2$ exactly, with no correction at any
  order in $|\vec k|$.
  A quartic deformation of the graviton dispersion is therefore not a consequence of this kernel.
  It follows that no coefficient of a quartic deformation is induced, hence no mapping of such a coefficient onto
  the pre-geometric scale $\ell_\chi$, and any bound on $\ell_\chi$ resting on that mapping does not hold.

  For $\beta \neq 0$ the second factor supplies a helicity-$2$ pole at $m_2^2 = -\cEH/\beta$ whose residue is
  opposite to the massless one. The opposite residue is the ghost character and is independent of the sign of
  $\cEH/\beta$; that sign fixes $m_2^2$ and hence whether the pole is also tachyonic. These are two distinct
  statements. The transverse-traceless calculation alone does not establish the full massive spin-$2$ multiplet,
  since the remaining sectors are not treated here; the resemblance to quadratic gravity is a comparison, not an
  identification.
  The finite values of $\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}$ and $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$, the signed mass squared $m_2^2$, and the
  presence of a massive scalar from the $R^2$ sector are matching data.

  \textbf{Interpretive status.}
  This is a negative result about a posited truncation, and its scope is exactly that.
  It removes a putative interferometric handle on $\ell_\chi$ and isolates what a genuine one would require ---
  beginning with a Lorentzian construction from which the kernel actually follows.
  }

  \noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Quadratic gravity, Graviton dispersion, Four-derivative truncation, Pole structure,

  \section{The operator considered}
    \label{sec:setup}

    The projective spectral action assigns to a Riemannian metric the renormalized functional
    \[
      \Spi[g] = \tfrac12\log\det{}'\Ag .
    \]
    A Lorentzian counterpart of this functional is not available: obtaining one requires an in-in construction on
    a closed time path, which is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}). The Lorentzian transverse-traceless kernel is
    therefore \emph{posited} here, not derived, and everything below is conditional on that posit.

    \begin{definition}[The posited kernel]
      \label{def:truncation}
      Assume that, linearized about Minkowski spacetime and restricted to the transverse-traceless sector, the
      local Lorentzian quadratic kernel truncated at four derivatives is
      \begin{equation}
        \OTT = \cEH\,\Box + \beta\,\Box^2 ,
        \label{eq:trunc}
      \end{equation}
      with $\cEH$ and $\beta$ real constants. Non-local form factors and all terms of order $\Box^3$ and higher
      are discarded.
    \end{definition}

    The results below therefore have the form: \emph{if} the local Lorentzian transverse-traceless kernel is
    $\Box(\cEH + \beta\Box)$, \emph{then} the stated conclusions follow. They are not statements about
    $\Spi$ itself, nor about the full renormalized kernel, and the difference matters wherever the discarded
    terms are not negligible --- in particular in the ultraviolet.

    \paragraph{Conventions.}
      We use signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and units with $c = 1$. For a plane wave $e^{ik\cdot x}$ with
      $k^\mu = (\omega, \vec k)$ we have $\Box \to -k^2$, where $k^2 = k^\mu k_\mu = -\omega^2 + |\vec k|^2$.

  \section{Solution structure}
    \label{sec:structure}

    Write $P = \Box$ and $Q = \cEH + \beta\Box$, so that $\OTT = PQ = QP$.

    Let $\mathcal{V}$ be any complex vector space of transverse-traceless tensor fields on which $\Box$ acts and
    which is stable under it --- smooth fields, or tempered distributions, indifferently. All kernels below are
    taken in $\mathcal{V}$. The argument is algebraic and uses no property of $\mathcal{V}$ beyond stability.

    \begin{proposition}[Direct-sum decomposition]
      \label{prop:decomposition}
      Let $\cEH \neq 0$. Then, in $\mathcal{V}$,
      \begin{equation}
        \ker \OTT \;=\; \ker P \,\oplus\, \ker Q ,
        \label{eq:directsum}
      \end{equation}
      and the decomposition of any $\hTT \in \ker\OTT$ as $\hTT = h_0 + h_m$ with $h_0 \in \ker P$ and
      $h_m \in \ker Q$ is unique.
      If in addition $\beta \neq 0$, then $\ker Q$ is characterized by $\Box h_m = -(\cEH/\beta)\,h_m$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The two factors satisfy the B\'ezout identity
      \begin{equation}
        Q - \beta P = \cEH ,
        \label{eq:bezout}
      \end{equation}
      a nonzero constant. For $\hTT \in \ker(PQ)$ set
      \[
        h_0 = \tfrac{1}{\cEH}\,Q\,\hTT ,
        \qquad
        h_m = -\tfrac{\beta}{\cEH}\,P\,\hTT .
      \]
      Then $h_0 + h_m = \tfrac{1}{\cEH}(Q - \beta P)\hTT = \hTT$ by~\eqref{eq:bezout};
      $P h_0 = \tfrac{1}{\cEH}PQ\,\hTT = 0$; and $Q h_m = -\tfrac{\beta}{\cEH}QP\,\hTT = 0$, using that $P$ and
      $Q$ commute. This gives $\ker\OTT = \ker P + \ker Q$.

      The sum is direct: if $h \in \ker P \cap \ker Q$ then $\cEH h = (Q - \beta P)h = 0$, so $h = 0$ because
      $\cEH \neq 0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[The kernel is not a union]
      \label{rem:not-union}
      Let $\cEH \neq 0$, let $\beta \neq 0$, and suppose $\ker Q \neq \{0\}$ in $\mathcal{V}$. The last is a
      hypothesis on $\mathcal{V}$ and not a consequence of $\beta \neq 0$: a stable space may fail to contain
      solutions of $Q h = 0$. It holds, for instance, in the tempered distributions, where $\ker Q$ carries the
      plane waves with $k^2 = \cEH/\beta$.
      Then $\ker\OTT$ is \emph{strictly} larger than $\ker P \cup \ker Q$: given nonzero $h_0 \in \ker P$ and
      $h_m \in \ker Q$, the sum
      $h_0 + h_m$ solves $\OTT h = 0$ while satisfying neither factor equation, since
      $\Box(h_0+h_m) = -(\cEH/\beta)h_m \neq 0$ and $(\cEH + \beta\Box)(h_0+h_m) = \cEH h_0 \neq 0$.
      A generic solution is a superposition of the two sectors, not a member of either.

      For $\beta = 0$ the operator reduces to $\cEH\Box$ and $\ker Q = \{0\}$: equation~\eqref{eq:directsum}
      still holds, trivially, and the union coincides with the kernel. The strictness above is a statement about
      $\beta \neq 0$.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[The degenerate case]
      \label{rem:degenerate}
      If $\cEH = 0$ the B\'ezout identity fails and Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition} does not apply:
      $\OTT = \beta\Box^2$, and $\ker\Box^2$ contains generalized solutions annihilated by $\Box^2$ but not by
      $\Box$. The decomposition into a massless and a massive sector is then unavailable.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Absence of a quartic graviton dispersion}
    \label{sec:no-k4}

    \begin{corollary}[Undeformed massless sector]
      \label{cor:no-k4}
      Let $\cEH \neq 0$. Under Definition~\ref{def:truncation}, the massless summand of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition} satisfies $\Box h_0 = 0$ exactly, hence for plane waves
      $h_0 \sim e^{ik\cdot x}$ one has $k^2 = 0$, i.e.
      \begin{equation}
        \omega^2 = |\vec k|^2 ,
        \label{eq:massless}
      \end{equation}
      with no correction at any order in $|\vec k|$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Immediate from~\eqref{eq:directsum}: the massless summand lies in $\ker\Box$ by construction, and $\ker\Box$
      does not depend on $\beta$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Why no quartic term appears]
      \label{rem:why}
      A relation $\omega^2 = |\vec k|^2 - \gamma\,\ell_\chi^2|\vec k|^4 + \mathcal{O}(|\vec k|^6)$ describes a
      single branch whose massless dispersion is deformed at quartic order. The kernel~\eqref{eq:trunc} has no
      such branch. Expanding $(\cEH + \beta z)^{-1}$ in powers of $z$ about $z = 0$ generates a series of terms
      analytic at the origin --- contact terms, in position space --- added to the massless pole; it does not
      move that pole, whose location is the zero of $\Box$ and is independent of $\beta$. Reading the resulting
      series as a deformed dispersion relation mistakes an analytic background for a shift of the pole.
    \end{remark}

    Gravitational-wave catalogues test modified dispersion through the phenomenological
    relation~\cite{LIGO2019Dispersion}
    \begin{equation}
      E^2 = p^2c^2 + A_\alpha\,p^\alpha c^\alpha ,
      \label{eq:lvk}
    \end{equation}
    constraining $A_\alpha$ for a range of exponents $\alpha$. The case $\alpha = 4$ is what a quartic deformation
    of the massless branch would populate; the case $\alpha = 0$ corresponds instead to a mass term, so the
    parametrization is not blind to massive branches.

    \begin{corollary}[No quartic coefficient, hence no scale bound through $\alpha=4$]
      \label{cor:no-bound}
      Under Definition~\ref{def:truncation} with $\cEH \neq 0$, Corollary~\ref{cor:no-k4} gives $A_4 = 0$ on the
      massless branch. There is therefore no mapping $A_4 \leftrightarrow \ell_\chi$, and any bound on $\ell_\chi$
      obtained by identifying an interferometric limit on $A_4$ with a quartic spectral coefficient does not hold.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{remark}[What is \emph{not} claimed]
      \label{rem:not-claimed}
      Corollary~\ref{cor:no-bound} does not say that these data cannot constrain this framework. The
      $\alpha = 0$ sector of~\eqref{eq:lvk} bears on massive branches, and the pole of Section~\ref{sec:poles} is
      one. Turning that into an actual constraint would require its mass, its coupling to the detector, and its
      excitation in the source --- none of which is supplied here (Section~\ref{sec:matching}). The negative
      result concerns the $A_4$ route only.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[No front-velocity claim]
      \label{rem:front}
      Equation~\eqref{eq:massless} is a statement about the truncation, not about signal fronts. A front velocity
      is governed by the large-$k$ behaviour of the full kernel, precisely where the discarded non-local and
      $\mathcal{O}(\Box^3)$ terms of Definition~\ref{def:truncation} are not negligible. No statement about front
      propagation is made here, in either direction.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Pole content}
    \label{sec:poles}

    Writing $z$ for the momentum-space image of $\Box$, the transverse-traceless propagator of~\eqref{eq:trunc}
    is, for $\cEH \neq 0$ \emph{and} $\beta \neq 0$ --- the partial fractions below divide by $\beta$ ---
    \begin{equation}
      \frac{1}{z(\cEH + \beta z)}
      = \frac{1}{\cEH\,z} \;-\; \frac{1}{\cEH\,(z + \cEH/\beta)} .
      \label{eq:partial}
    \end{equation}

    Two facts follow, and they are independent of one another.

    \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
      \item \textbf{Ghost character.} The two residues in~\eqref{eq:partial} have opposite signs, whatever the
            signs of $\cEH$ and $\beta$. The second pole is therefore a ghost relative to the massless one. This
            does not depend on $\cEH/\beta$.
      \item \textbf{Tachyonic character.} The second pole sits at $z = -\cEH/\beta$, so the sign of $\cEH/\beta$
            fixes the sign of the corresponding mass squared, and with it whether the mode is tachyonic. This is a
            separate question from~(i) and has a separate answer.
    \end{enumerate}

    For $\beta \neq 0$ the second pole sits at $k^2 = \cEH/\beta$, that is at
    \begin{equation}
      m_2^2 = -\,\frac{\cEH}{\beta} ,
      \label{eq:mass}
    \end{equation}
    in the conventions of Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

    This resembles the pole content of quadratic gravity, where a massless graviton is accompanied by a massive
    spin-$2$ ghost~\cite{Stelle1977,Calmet2017}. The resemblance is a comparison and not an identification: the
    transverse-traceless calculation establishes a helicity-$2$ pole with opposite residue, whereas the massive
    spin-$2$ multiplet is a statement about all five polarizations. The scalar and vector sectors are not treated
    here (Section~\ref{sec:open}), so the full multiplet structure is not established.

    \begin{remark}[The scalar sector is not settled]
      \label{rem:scalar}
      The minimal $a_4$ carries an $R^2$ sector alongside $C^2$. In the effective action at second order in
      curvature this generically supports a massive scalar in addition to the massive spin-2
      mode~\cite{Calmet2017}. Excluding it requires a matching condition on the $R^2$ coefficient. The mode
      content of the truncation is therefore not fully determined here.
    \end{remark}

  \section{What is matching data}
    \label{sec:matching}

    \begin{remark}[Status of $\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}$ and $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$]
      \label{rem:matching}
      The Seeley--DeWitt coefficient $a_4$ controls a logarithmic contribution to the running of the
      four-derivative couplings. It does not fix the finite value of $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$.

      The normalization and sign of $\tfrac12\log\det{}'\Ag$ are not at issue: they are fixed by the definition of
      the functional. What leaves $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$ undetermined is the finite parts of the counterterms, the
      complete operator content, the continuation to Lorentzian signature, and the renormalization
      condition~\cite{Vassilevich2003}. An identification $\beta = -2\alpha_C$ read off the Weyl-squared part of
      $a_4$ supplies none of these, and is not a determination of $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$.

      The same holds for $\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}$. Consequently the signed mass squared
      \[
        m_2^2 = -\,\frac{\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}}{\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}}
      \]
      of Section~\ref{sec:poles} --- equivalently the pole scale $|\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}/\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}|$
      together with its sign --- is a matching datum, and is not derivably tied to $\ell_\chi$.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Open problems}
    \label{sec:open}

    \paragraph{The in-in construction.}
      A Schwinger--Keldysh effective action is built from doubled field configurations on a closed time path, with
      the branches coupled by the boundary condition at the turning point~\cite{Jordan1986,Calzetta2008}.
      Substituting a retarded Green operator for $\Ag^{-1}$ in an ordinary single-field Hessian is not that
      construction and does not follow from it. Whether the projective spectral action admits a closed-time-path
      formulation, and whether the quadratic form it would yield agrees with~\eqref{eq:trunc}, is open.

    \paragraph{The vector sector.}
      The linearized vector equation carries a $\Box\bar h_{0i}$ term, hence a second time derivative that
      harmonic gauge does not remove. Only under a stationarity assumption does it reduce to an elliptic
      constraint. A derivation from the full linear system with spin projectors, and the fate of the
      four-derivative correction within it, are open.

    \paragraph{Beyond the truncation.}
      Definition~\ref{def:truncation} discards non-local form factors and $\mathcal{O}(\Box^3)$ terms. Whether the
      absence of a quartic graviton dispersion survives their inclusion is not addressed here.

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    Under the posited kernel of Definition~\ref{def:truncation}, and for $\cEH \neq 0$, the solution space is the
    direct sum $\ker\Box \oplus \ker(\cEH + \beta\Box)$; the second summand is a massive sector when
    $\beta \neq 0$, and trivial otherwise. The massless summand is undeformed: its dispersion is
    $\omega^2 = |\vec k|^2$ with no correction at any order in $|\vec k|$.

    No quartic deformation of the graviton dispersion follows, hence $A_4 = 0$ on the massless branch and no
    mapping $A_4 \leftrightarrow \ell_\chi$. Any bound on $\ell_\chi$ resting on that identification does not
    hold. This does not exhaust the catalogue: the $\alpha = 0$ sector of~\eqref{eq:lvk} bears on massive
    branches, and constraining the pole of Section~\ref{sec:poles} through it would require the inputs listed in
    Remark~\ref{rem:not-claimed}. The negative result concerns the $A_4$ route.

    For $\beta \neq 0$ the accompanying pole is a ghost by virtue of its residue, and tachyonic or not according
    to the sign of $\cEH/\beta$ --- two distinct statements. What is established is a helicity-$2$ pole in the
    transverse-traceless sector; the five-polarization massive spin-$2$ multiplet of quadratic gravity is a
    comparison, not a conclusion, since the scalar and vector sectors are untreated. Its signed mass squared, the
    finite coefficients that fix it, and whether an $R^2$ scalar joins it are matching data supplied from outside
    the truncation.

    \subsection{Interpretive outlook}
      \label{subsec:outlook}

      \textbf{Interpretation, not derived result.}
      In its transverse-traceless sector, the posited kernel resembles quadratic gravity. It is tempting to read
      that resemblance as support for the spectral construction; the reading is available but weak, since any
      construction generating $C^2$ and $R^2$ sectors would resemble it equally well --- and the resemblance is
      established here only for one helicity-$2$ pole, not for the full mode content. The defensible statement is
      narrower: the posited kernel is compatible with the known infrared structure and predicts nothing beyond it
      that is presently observable.

      What this note contributes is a boundary. Several inputs are missing before gravitational waves can test
      this framework at all, and they are not interchangeable: a Lorentzian construction from which the kernel of
      Definition~\ref{def:truncation} actually follows, rather than being posited; a renormalization condition
      fixing $m_2^2$; the coupling of that mode to a detector and its excitation in a source; and a treatment of
      the scalar and vector sectors sufficient to settle the mode content. Naming them is what makes them
      addressable, and none of them is supplied here.
