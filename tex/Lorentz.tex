  \title{No Quartic Graviton Dispersion from the Local Covariant\\Four-Derivative Truncation}

  \author{J\'{e}r\^{o}me Beau\\
  \small Independent Researcher, France\\
  \small \texttt{jerome.beau@cosmochrony.org}}

  \maketitle

  \abstract{
  This note settles one question about the local covariant four-derivative truncation of the projective spectral
  action: whether it deforms the dispersion of the graviton.
  It does not.

  In the transverse-traceless sector the truncated quadratic operator factorizes as
  $\OTT = \Box\,(\cEH + \beta\Box)$.
  When $\cEH \neq 0$ the two factors are coprime, and the solution space is the \emph{direct sum}
  $\ker\Box \oplus \ker(\cEH + \beta\Box)$: every solution decomposes uniquely as a massless part plus a massive
  part, and a generic solution satisfies neither factor equation on its own.
  The massless summand obeys $\omega^2 = c^2k^2$ within the truncation, with no correction at any order in $k$.
  A quartic deformation $\omega^2 = c^2k^2 - \gamma\,\ell_\chi^2k^4$ of the graviton dispersion is therefore not a
  consequence of this operator, and there is no mapping onto the $A_4$ parametrization used in gravitational-wave
  catalogues, hence no bound on $\ell_\chi$ from interferometric dispersion data.

  The second factor supplies a pole with residue opposite to the massless one --- the ghost character, which is
  independent of the sign of $\cEH/\beta$ --- located at a mass squared whose sign, and therefore whether it is
  also tachyonic, is a separate question. This is the known content of quadratic gravity.
  The finite values of $\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}$ and $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$, the pole mass, and the presence of a
  massive scalar from the $R^2$ sector are matching data, not outputs of the truncation.

  \textbf{Interpretive status.}
  This is a negative result about a truncation, and its scope is exactly that.
  It removes a putative interferometric handle on the pre-geometric scale and isolates what a genuine one would
  require: a condition fixing the pole mass, and an observable sensitive to a massive companion rather than to a
  deformed massless branch.
  }

  \noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Quadratic gravity, Graviton dispersion, Four-derivative truncation, Pole structure,
  Ghost residue, Renormalization matching, Heat-kernel expansion, Effective field theory

  \section{The operator considered}
    \label{sec:setup}

    The projective spectral action assigns to a metric the renormalized functional
    \[
      \Spi[g] = \tfrac12\log\det{}'\Ag .
    \]
    Its metric variation carries a local Einstein term together with higher-derivative local terms, non-local
    form factors, and contributions of higher order in $\Box$.

    This note concerns a truncation of that structure, and the truncation is an assumption, not a result.

    \begin{definition}[Local covariant four-derivative TT truncation]
      \label{def:truncation}
      Linearize about Minkowski spacetime and restrict to the transverse-traceless sector. Retain the local terms
      of the quadratic form up to four derivatives, and discard the non-local form factors and all terms of order
      $\Box^3$ and higher. The resulting operator is
      \begin{equation}
        \OTT = \cEH\,\Box + \beta\,\Box^2 ,
        \label{eq:trunc}
      \end{equation}
      with $\cEH$ and $\beta$ constants.
    \end{definition}

    Every statement below is a statement about~\eqref{eq:trunc}. None of them is a statement about the full
    renormalized kernel, and the difference matters wherever the discarded terms are not negligible --- in
    particular in the ultraviolet.

  \section{Solution structure}
    \label{sec:structure}

    Write $P = \Box$ and $Q = \cEH + \beta\Box$, so that $\OTT = PQ = QP$.

    \begin{proposition}[Direct-sum decomposition]
      \label{prop:decomposition}
      Let $\cEH \neq 0$. Then
      \begin{equation}
        \ker \OTT \;=\; \ker P \,\oplus\, \ker Q ,
        \label{eq:directsum}
      \end{equation}
      and the decomposition of any $\hTT \in \ker\OTT$ is unique:
      $\hTT = h_0 + h_m$ with $\Box h_0 = 0$ and $\Box h_m = -(\cEH/\beta)\,h_m$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The two factors satisfy the B\'ezout identity
      \begin{equation}
        Q - \beta P = \cEH ,
        \label{eq:bezout}
      \end{equation}
      a nonzero constant. For $\hTT \in \ker(PQ)$ set
      \[
        h_0 = \tfrac{1}{\cEH}\,Q\,\hTT ,
        \qquad
        h_m = -\tfrac{\beta}{\cEH}\,P\,\hTT .
      \]
      Then $h_0 + h_m = \tfrac{1}{\cEH}(Q - \beta P)\hTT = \hTT$ by~\eqref{eq:bezout};
      $P h_0 = \tfrac{1}{\cEH}PQ\,\hTT = 0$; and $Q h_m = -\tfrac{\beta}{\cEH}QP\,\hTT = 0$, using that $P$ and
      $Q$ commute. This gives $\ker\OTT = \ker P + \ker Q$.

      The sum is direct: if $h \in \ker P \cap \ker Q$ then $\cEH h = (Q - \beta P)h = 0$, so $h = 0$ because
      $\cEH \neq 0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[The kernel is not a union]
      \label{rem:not-union}
      $\ker\OTT$ is strictly larger than $\ker P \cup \ker Q$. Given any nonzero $h_0 \in \ker P$ and
      $h_m \in \ker Q$, the sum $h_0 + h_m$ solves $\OTT h = 0$ while satisfying neither factor equation:
      $\Box(h_0+h_m) = -(\cEH/\beta)h_m \neq 0$ and $(\cEH + \beta\Box)(h_0+h_m) = \cEH h_0 \neq 0$.
      A generic solution is therefore a superposition of the two sectors, not a member of one of them.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[The degenerate case]
      \label{rem:degenerate}
      If $\cEH = 0$ the B\'ezout identity fails and Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition} does not apply:
      $\OTT = \beta\Box^2$, and $\ker\Box^2$ contains generalized solutions annihilated by $\Box^2$ but not by
      $\Box$. The decomposition into a massless and a massive sector is then unavailable.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Absence of a quartic graviton dispersion}
    \label{sec:no-k4}

    \begin{corollary}[Undeformed massless sector]
      \label{cor:no-k4}
      Let $\cEH \neq 0$. Within the truncation~\eqref{eq:trunc}, the massless summand of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition} satisfies $\Box h_0 = 0$ exactly, hence for plane waves
      $h_0 \sim e^{ik\cdot x}$,
      \begin{equation}
        \omega^2 = c^2 k^2 ,
        \label{eq:massless}
      \end{equation}
      with no correction at any order in $k$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Immediate from~\eqref{eq:directsum}: the massless summand lies in $\ker\Box$ by construction, and $\ker\Box$
      does not depend on $\beta$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Why no quartic term appears]
      \label{rem:why}
      A relation $\omega^2 = c^2k^2 - \gamma\,\ell_\chi^2k^4 + \mathcal{O}(k^6)$ describes a single branch whose
      massless dispersion is deformed at quartic order. The truncation has no such branch. Expanding
      $(1 + (\beta/\cEH)\Box)^{-1}$ in powers of $\Box$ rearranges the residues of the propagator; it does not
      move the zero of $\Box$, which is where the massless pole sits. The quartic coefficient obtained that way is
      an artefact of treating a two-pole propagator as one deformed pole.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{corollary}[No interferometric dispersion bound]
      \label{cor:no-bound}
      Gravitational-wave catalogues constrain $\omega^2 = c^2k^2 + A_4k^4$ on the propagating
      graviton~\cite{LIGO2019Dispersion}. By Corollary~\ref{cor:no-k4} the truncation induces no such $A_4$.
      There is consequently no mapping of this construction onto that parametrization, and no bound on $\ell_\chi$
      from interferometric dispersion data.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{remark}[No front-velocity claim]
      \label{rem:front}
      Equation~\eqref{eq:massless} is a statement about the truncation, not about signal fronts. A front velocity
      is governed by the large-$k$ behaviour of the full kernel, precisely where the discarded non-local and
      $\mathcal{O}(\Box^3)$ terms of Definition~\ref{def:truncation} are not negligible. No statement about front
      propagation is made here, in either direction.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Pole content}
    \label{sec:poles}

    Writing $z$ for the momentum-space image of $\Box$, the transverse-traceless propagator of~\eqref{eq:trunc}
    is, for $\cEH \neq 0$,
    \begin{equation}
      \frac{1}{z(\cEH + \beta z)}
      = \frac{1}{\cEH}\left( \frac{1}{z} - \frac{1}{z + \cEH/\beta} \right).
      \label{eq:partial}
    \end{equation}

    Two facts follow, and they are independent of one another.

    \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
      \item \textbf{Ghost character.} The two residues in~\eqref{eq:partial} have opposite signs, whatever the
            signs of $\cEH$ and $\beta$. The second pole is therefore a ghost relative to the massless one. This
            does not depend on $\cEH/\beta$.
      \item \textbf{Tachyonic character.} The second pole sits at $z = -\cEH/\beta$, so the sign of $\cEH/\beta$
            fixes the sign of the corresponding mass squared, and with it whether the mode is tachyonic. This is a
            separate question from~(i) and has a separate answer.
    \end{enumerate}

    This is the standard pole content of quadratic gravity: a massless graviton accompanied by a massive spin-2
    ghost~\cite{Stelle1977,Calmet2017}. The truncation reproduces a known structure.

    \begin{remark}[The scalar sector is not settled]
      \label{rem:scalar}
      The minimal $a_4$ carries an $R^2$ sector alongside $C^2$. In the effective action at second order in
      curvature this generically supports a massive scalar in addition to the massive spin-2
      mode~\cite{Calmet2017}. Excluding it requires a matching condition on the $R^2$ coefficient. The mode
      content of the truncation is therefore not fully determined here.
    \end{remark}

  \section{What is matching data}
    \label{sec:matching}

    \begin{remark}[Status of $\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}$ and $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$]
      \label{rem:matching}
      The Seeley--DeWitt coefficient $a_4$ controls a logarithmic contribution to the running of the
      four-derivative couplings. It does not fix the finite value of $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$, which depends in
      addition on the overall normalization and sign of the determinant and on the renormalization prescription.
      An identification $\beta = -2\alpha_C$ read off the Weyl-squared part of $a_4$ omits all three, and is not
      a determination of $\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$.

      The same holds for $\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}$: its finite value and sign are fixed by a renormalization
      condition together with the complete operator content~\cite{Vassilevich2003}. Consequently the pole mass
      $\cEH^{\mathrm{ren}}/\beta^{\mathrm{ren}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:poles} is a matching datum, and is not
      derivably tied to $\ell_\chi$.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Open problems}
    \label{sec:open}

    \paragraph{The in-in construction.}
      A Schwinger--Keldysh effective action is built from doubled field configurations on a closed time path, with
      the branches coupled by the boundary condition at the turning point~\cite{Jordan1986,Calzetta2008}.
      Substituting a retarded Green operator for $\Ag^{-1}$ in an ordinary single-field Hessian is not that
      construction and does not follow from it. Whether the projective spectral action admits a closed-time-path
      formulation, and whether the quadratic form it would yield agrees with~\eqref{eq:trunc}, is open.

    \paragraph{The vector sector.}
      The linearized vector equation carries a $\Box\bar h_{0i}$ term, hence a second time derivative that
      harmonic gauge does not remove. Only under a stationarity assumption does it reduce to an elliptic
      constraint. A derivation from the full linear system with spin projectors, and the fate of the
      four-derivative correction within it, are open.

    \paragraph{Beyond the truncation.}
      Definition~\ref{def:truncation} discards non-local form factors and $\mathcal{O}(\Box^3)$ terms. Whether the
      absence of a quartic graviton dispersion survives their inclusion is not addressed here.

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    Within the local covariant four-derivative truncation of the transverse-traceless sector, and for
    $\cEH \neq 0$, the solution space is the direct sum of a massless and a massive sector. The massless sector is
    undeformed: its dispersion is $\omega^2 = c^2k^2$ with no correction at any order in $k$. No quartic
    deformation of the graviton dispersion follows, hence no $A_4$ coefficient, and no bound on $\ell_\chi$ from
    gravitational-wave dispersion data.

    The accompanying pole is a ghost by virtue of its residue, and tachyonic or not according to the sign of
    $\cEH/\beta$ --- two distinct statements. Its mass, the finite coefficients that fix it, and whether an $R^2$
    scalar joins it are matching data supplied from outside the truncation.

    \subsection{Interpretive outlook}
      \label{subsec:outlook}

      \textbf{Interpretation, not derived result.}
      The truncation lands on the known pole content of quadratic gravity. It is tempting to read that agreement
      as support for the spectral construction; the reading is available but weak, since any construction
      generating $C^2$ and $R^2$ sectors would agree equally well. The defensible statement is narrower: the
      truncation is compatible with the known infrared structure and predicts nothing beyond it that is presently
      observable.

      What this note contributes is a boundary. Two inputs must be supplied before gravitational waves can test
      this framework at all: a condition fixing the pole mass, and an observable sensitive to a massive companion
      rather than to a deformed massless branch. Naming them is what makes them addressable.
